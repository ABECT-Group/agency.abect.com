Google Ads is one of the fastest ways to get customers from the internet: someone searches for "website development" or "washing machine repair Lviv" — and within seconds they see your offer. But precisely because it is so easy to launch, many businesses lose money: you can create a campaign in 15 minutes, but setting it up so it pays off is much harder.

In this guide we'll look at the campaign types available, when to trust Google's automation, and which mistakes most often turn advertising into a "black hole" for your budget.

## Before you launch: what needs to be ready

Advertising won't fix a weak offer or an inconvenient website — it only brings people to it faster. Before you start, check three things:

- **Landing page.** It should match the search query, load quickly on a phone and have a clear action: leave a request, call, buy.
- **Conversion tracking.** Without it you won't know which ads bring leads, and automated bidding strategies simply can't learn. At minimum — a form submission event and a phone click event via Google Tag Manager or GA4.
- **Unit economics.** Work out how much you can pay for one lead and still stay profitable. That number is the benchmark for every decision that follows.

## Campaign types and when each one works

### Search campaigns

Classic paid search: text ads above the search results. You pay per click, and the ad is shown in response to a specific query — meaning the person is already looking for your product.

Search campaigns suit almost anyone selling services or products with existing demand. This is where to start if your budget is limited: you pay for a "hot" audience.

### Display campaigns

Banners and responsive ads on Google partner websites, in mobile apps and Gmail. The audience here is "colder": the person is reading the news or checking the weather, not looking for your product.

Display advertising works well for brand awareness and **remarketing** — showing ads again to people who have already visited your site but didn't leave a request. It's not the best main source of leads at the start.

### Video campaigns (YouTube)

Ads before videos, in the YouTube feed and in Shorts. They suit products that need to be shown: interiors, gadgets, training, events. Video builds trust but rarely drives "here and now" sales — evaluate it together with other channels.

### Performance Max

A campaign in which Google distributes your ads across Search, YouTube, Display, Gmail, Maps and Discover on its own. You provide texts, images, videos and conversion goals, and the algorithm finds where they perform best.

Performance Max can deliver great results for online stores and businesses with a high volume of conversions. But there's a catch: it's a "black box", and without properly configured conversions the system will optimise for random actions. Launch it once you have a steady flow of leads and a clear conversion value.

### Shopping and Demand Gen campaigns

Product ads with a photo and price are a must-have for online stores: they pull data from Google Merchant Center. Demand Gen is a format for building demand on YouTube, Discover and Gmail when a product is new and people aren't searching for it yet.

## Smart Bidding: when to trust automation

Smart Bidding strategies let an algorithm set the bid for every auction based on device, time, location, user history and other signals. The main options:

- **Maximize conversions** — get as many conversions as possible within your daily budget.
- **Target cost per acquisition (tCPA)** — keep the average cost per lead close to a set amount.
- **Maximize conversion value** and **target return on ad spend (tROAS)** — for stores where the total sales value matters more than the number of orders.

Automation only works well when it has something to learn from. A practical rule: switching to target strategies makes sense when a campaign consistently collects at least a few dozen conversions a month. Before that, it's reasonable to start with manual bidding or "Maximize clicks" with a maximum CPC limit, then move to "Maximize conversions".

After every strategy change the system goes through a learning period — usually 1–2 weeks. Don't change settings every day: the algorithm won't have time to stabilise.

## How not to waste your budget: common mistakes

### 1. Broad match without negative keywords

The keyword "website" can show your ad for "job website", "free download website" or "how to build a website yourself". Review the "Search terms" report regularly and add irrelevant words to your negative list: "free", "jobs", "yourself", "course" — depending on your niche.

### 2. One campaign for everything

When all services are grouped into one ad group, the ad copy becomes generic and the budget drifts to the cheapest rather than the most profitable queries. Split campaigns by service line and ad groups by user intent.

### 3. Sending traffic to the homepage

Someone searched for "landing page for ads" but landed on the homepage, where they still have to find the right service. Every ad group should lead to a page that matches that exact query.

### 4. Missing or duplicated conversions

If a conversion is counted twice (for example, both the thank-you page view and the form event), reports show results twice as good as reality, and Smart Bidding optimises on false data. Check conversions with a test request after every change on the site.

### 5. Showing ads to "the whole world"

By default Google may show ads to people who are merely interested in your region. If you operate locally, choose to show ads to people located in that region in the location settings.

### 6. Judging results after one week

The first days are testing. Draw conclusions once the campaign has enough clicks and conversions for a meaningful difference, not after the first 20 visits.

## Campaign launch checklist

1. Define your target cost per lead or sale.
2. Set up and verify conversion tracking.
3. Prepare relevant landing pages for each service line.
4. Collect keywords and an initial negative keyword list.
5. Create separate campaigns for search and remarketing.
6. Write 2–3 ad variations per group and add assets: phone, callouts, sitelinks.
7. For the first 2–4 weeks, clean up search terms daily, then weekly.

## Summary

Effective Google Ads isn't "launch and forget" — it's a system: the right campaign type for the task, accurate conversions, relevant pages and regular optimisation. Start with search campaigns for "hot" queries, set up analytics, and only then scale with Performance Max and display advertising.
