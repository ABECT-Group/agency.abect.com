A common myth: paid search is a game for large companies with budgets in the thousands. In reality, a small business can get leads from Google Ads for $5–10 a day. The key is not to try to cover everything at once, but to focus your money where it pays back fastest.

## How much money you actually need

The minimum budget depends not on what you'd like, but on two numbers: the **cost per click** in your niche and your website's **conversion rate** (the share of visitors who leave a request).

Example calculation:

- average cost per click — $0.40;
- daily budget — $6, which is about 15 clicks a day;
- landing page conversion rate — 5%.

On average that gives 0.75 leads a day, or 20–23 leads a month for about $180. If your average order value and margin allow you to pay $8–9 per lead, the ads pay off.

In niches where a click costs $3–5 (legal services, real estate, medicine in big cities), however, $5 a day is just one or two clicks. A small budget there won't give you enough data to draw conclusions, so it's better to start with a narrower segment: one district, one service or very specific queries.

### How to find out the cost per click before launch

Google Ads Keyword Planner shows the forecast bid range for "top of page" for your keywords and region. The numbers are approximate, but they give you a sense of the spend you're looking at.

## Prioritising channels: where the first $5 should go

When the budget is limited, every dollar should go to the warmest audience. The recommended order:

1. **Search campaigns for "hot" queries.** Queries with purchase intent: "order", "price", "buy", service name + your city. People are already looking for a solution — you just need to offer yours.
2. **Brand queries.** If people already search for your company by name, protect those impressions — clicks are cheap and conversion is high.
3. **Remarketing.** Showing ads again to people who have visited your site. The audience is small, so the cost is minimal, and bringing visitors back often adds extra leads.
4. **Display, video, Performance Max** — once the first three consistently bring leads and the budget can grow.

### What to switch off so you don't lose money

- **Search partners** and **Display Network** in a search campaign — at the start it's better to keep Google Search only.
- Showing ads to "people interested in your location" — for a local business, keep only people physically in your city.
- Night-time ads if you don't answer calls and requests until morning (set in the ad schedule).

## How to set up a campaign on a small budget

- **Few keywords, but precise ones.** 10–20 targeted phrases in phrase or exact match beat 200 broad ones.
- **Negative keywords from day one.** "Free", "DIY", "jobs", "employee reviews" — the list depends on your niche, but you always need one.
- **One campaign — one service line.** If you sell two services with different margins, separate them to control each budget.
- **Manual bidding or "Maximize clicks" with a CPC limit.** Automated conversion strategies need data you don't have yet at the start with a small budget.
- **Ad assets.** Phone, location, callouts and sitelinks are free but increase the ad's size and click-through rate.

## Fast payback: typical scenarios

Below are hypothetical calculations to illustrate the logic. Your numbers will depend on your niche, region and website.

### Local services (repairs, cleaning, beauty salon)

Budget $6/day, click ~$0.30, conversion 6%. Roughly 20 clicks and 1–1.5 leads a day. With an average order of $40 and half the leads closing, the monthly spend of ~$180 is recovered within the first 10–15 orders.

### B2B service with a high order value

Budget $10/day, click ~$1, conversion 4%. Around 12 leads a month. Even one or two signed contracts worth $1,000+ pay for several months of advertising — but the sales cycle is longer, so evaluate results after 2–3 months.

### Small online store

Budget $8/day on search and Shopping ads for the most popular items. The key metric here is return on ad spend (ROAS): sales must exceed costs, taking margin into account. Start with products that have the best margin and demand.

## When to increase the budget

Scale only when:

- the cost per lead has been stable for 2–4 weeks and works for you;
- the campaign is often limited by budget (Google shows the "Limited by budget" status);
- your sales team can keep up with incoming leads.

Increase the budget gradually — by 20–30% at a time — so you don't disrupt optimisation.

## Summary

$5 a day isn't that little if you spend it precisely: search for "hot" queries, strict negative keywords, local targeting and a page that converts. Most losses on small budgets come not from too little money, but from spreading it across every channel at once.
