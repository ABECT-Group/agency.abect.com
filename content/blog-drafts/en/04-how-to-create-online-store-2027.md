Launching an online store today is easier than ever: platforms offer ready-made templates, payment services can be connected in a day, and delivery services have APIs for automation. But between "I've built a website" and "I've made my first sales" there are many decisions that affect costs, growth speed and even legal risks.

This guide is a step-by-step plan, from choosing a platform to your first orders.

## Step 1. Define your model and niche

Before choosing technology, answer a few questions:

- **What are you selling and to whom?** A few products you make yourself or a catalogue of thousands of items from suppliers.
- **Where does the stock come from?** Your own warehouse, dropshipping, made-to-order production.
- **Where are your customers?** Ukraine only, or also the EU and the US — this determines payments, delivery and site languages.
- **What's your margin?** It determines how much you can spend on advertising and delivery.

## Step 2. WooCommerce vs Shopify vs a custom solution

### WooCommerce

A free WordPress plugin and the most widely used e-commerce platform in the world.

- **Pros:** flexibility, thousands of plugins, full control over the site and data, many Ukrainian integrations (payment providers, Nova Poshta, Ukrposhta).
- **Cons:** you need hosting and technical support; lots of plugins slow the site down and create security risks if they aren't updated.
- **Best for:** small and medium businesses that need flexibility without large upfront costs.

### Shopify

A SaaS platform: the store works "out of the box", hosting and updates are handled by the service.

- **Pros:** fast launch, stability, a convenient admin panel, a large app store, strong for selling abroad.
- **Cons:** monthly subscription and extra fees; some Ukrainian payment systems and delivery services can only be connected through third-party apps; limited customisation of business logic.
- **Best for:** brands selling to international markets and those who want minimal technical hassle.

### Custom development

A store built around your business — for example, on Next.js with a headless CMS.

- **Pros:** maximum loading speed, unique design, any logic (B2B pricing, configurators, complex discounts), integration with your accounting system.
- **Cons:** higher cost and longer development time at the start.
- **Best for:** businesses with complex processes, a large catalogue or plans to scale, where performance and SEO directly affect sales.

### There's another option: website builders

Builders such as Weblium or Horoshop let you launch a small store in a few days without a developer. It's a good way to test a niche before investing in a full-scale platform.

## Step 3. What you need to launch

### Products and content

- High-quality photos from several angles on a plain background.
- Unique descriptions with specifications, sizes and materials — not copies from the supplier's site (that hurts SEO).
- A catalogue structure with filters that shoppers actually use.

### Accepting payments

At minimum — online card payments (internet acquiring), Apple Pay / Google Pay and, for Ukraine, cash on delivery. More on choosing a payment provider in our article comparing Monobank, LiqPay, Portmone and Stripe.

### Delivery

- Integration with Nova Poshta and Ukrposhta: automatic branch selection, cost calculation, creating waybills from the admin panel.
- Clear delivery and returns terms on a separate page.
- For international sales — services with tracking and clear delivery times.

### Order processing

Who confirms orders, and how fast? Set up new-order notifications in Telegram or by email, plus automatic status emails to customers.

## Step 4. Legal aspects of e-commerce in Ukraine

This section is a general overview, not legal advice. Consult a lawyer or accountant before launching.

- **Business form.** Most often a sole proprietor (FOP). It's important to choose the right single-tax group and activity codes (KVED) that allow online retail.
- **Fiscal receipts.** When accepting card payments or cash on delivery you usually need to issue a fiscal receipt — a software cash register (PRRO) is used for this. Check whether it's required in your case.
- **The Law "On Electronic Commerce".** It regulates online contracts: the site must have a public offer with terms of sale, delivery and payment.
- **Consumer protection.** For distance purchases, customers have the right to return goods of proper quality within 14 days (with exceptions for certain categories). Return terms must be described on the site.
- **Personal data.** A privacy policy, consent to data processing in forms, a secure connection (HTTPS).
- **Seller information.** The sole proprietor's or company's name, contacts and address must be easy to find on the site.

## Step 5. Your first sales

A website without traffic doesn't sell. The fastest sources of first orders:

- **Google Shopping and search ads** — for products with existing demand.
- **Marketplaces** (Rozetka, Prom, Epicentr) — a parallel channel that brings sales even before the site gains SEO traffic.
- **Social media** — for products that "show" well visually.
- **SEO** — a long-term investment: optimised categories and useful articles bring free traffic for months.

Be sure to set up e-commerce tracking in GA4: you'll see which channels bring real sales, not just visitors.

## Summary

There's no "best" platform — there's the one that fits your scale, market and budget. A builder works for testing an idea, WooCommerce for a flexible start, Shopify for international sales, and a custom solution for complex logic and scale. The main thing is to think through payments, delivery and legal matters before launch, not after the first customer complaints.
